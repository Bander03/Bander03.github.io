## Rejection notes

**Hero background image — rejected the whole category, generated zero candidates.**
The assignment's own rule is "design frames the work, it never steals the show." A hero photo or
illustration behind the through-line sentence adds visual noise competing with the one sentence
that's supposed to land first, and it would be the only AI-touched pixel on a page whose entire
argument is "trust the real numbers." Generating options and picking the least-bad one would still
mean shipping a decorative AI image on a page about not overclaiming AI's output. The plain accent
background with the wordmark does the job with zero risk of undercutting the site's own claim.

**About-section photo — rejected AI generation, used a labeled placeholder instead.**
A generated headshot is the single worst place to use AI on a portfolio: it's the one image
visitors implicitly assume is real, and if it looks slightly synthetic (skin texture, asymmetric
ears, background artifacts — the classic tells) it undermines trust in every other claim on the
page, including the model numbers. The honest move is a plain placeholder with real name/role text
and a note to swap in an actual photo, not a synthetic stand-in that quietly misrepresents what's
real. Swap this the moment a real photo exists.

**Chart restyling — considered asking AI to "reskin" the charts to match the brand palette, rejected.**
The charts are proof of a real, honest result (Precision@50 0.24 → 0.74). Regenerating them in a
prettier style — even from the same underlying numbers — adds a step where something could
silently drift from what the pipeline actually produced. Keeping them in their native
pipeline-generated style, exactly as `outputs/model_report.md` shipped them, is what makes them
verifiable: anyone can re-run `scripts/04_evaluate_and_export.py` and get the same SVGs.

## What this leaves as the AI-image footprint of the whole site

Zero generated images. Every visual is either a real pipeline output or a two-shape hand-built
SVG mark. That's the point of the exercise: the skill being practiced is deciding *not* to
generate, not just picking a favorite among generated options.
